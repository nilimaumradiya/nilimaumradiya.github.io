Phthalate Concentration Analysis in Water Samples

An end-to-end Data Analyst case study using Python, Pandas, SciPy and Power BI to clean, analyze and visualize spatial, seasonal and data-quality patterns across 80 water samples.

The Challenge

The objective was to turn a small but messy environmental dataset into a reliable analytical story that could be explored by location, season, water category and chemical.

Data challenges

  • Special value 9999 needed to be treated as missing rather than a real concentration.
  • Chemical completeness varied substantially, especially for BBP.
  • DEHP and DnBP contained extreme values that heavily skewed the mean.
  • The dataset needed consistent categories for analysis and Power BI.

Analytical questions

  • Which chemicals have the greatest variability?
  • Do concentrations differ by season or location?
  • Do drinking-water and overhead-water samples differ?
  • Where are the strongest data-quality gaps and extreme observations?

The Solution

I built the analysis as a complete analytics workflow rather than treating the dashboard as the final step.

Statistical Analysis

Season

Summer median sample total: 1.093 µg/L. Winter median: 8.032 µg/L.

Mann–Whitney U = 334, p < 0.001. Winter median was approximately 7.4× Summer.

Study area

Area medians were Bhongdwar 1.937, Lalpur 1.274, Ramnagra 8.032 and Ranjhi 0.951 µg/L.

Kruskal–Wallis H = 9.61, p = 0.022, indicating overall spatial heterogeneity.

Water category

Drinking-water median: approximately 1.65 µg/L. Overhead-water median: approximately 1.86 µg/L.

Mann–Whitney U = 822, p = 0.406.

Post-hoc requirement

Dunn’s pairwise test should be run on the original sample-level totals after the significant Kruskal–Wallis result.

The available project HTML does not contain those pairwise results, so this page does not invent them.

Insights & Recommendations

Insight 01

DEHP dominates the measured concentration

DEHP contributes approximately 92.8% of aggregate valid measured concentration. Recommendation: prioritize DEHP monitoring while retaining multi-chemical screening.

Insight 02

Seasonality is a major analytical dimension

Winter median is approximately 7.4× Summer. Recommendation: incorporate seasonal monitoring and capture source/storage metadata.

Insight 03

Location-level differences need follow-up

The Kruskal–Wallis result is significant. Recommendation: perform Dunn’s pairwise testing and targeted resampling.

Insight 04

Data completeness affects interpretation

BBP has only 18.8% valid observations. Recommendation: improve analytical coverage before making strong BBP comparisons.
